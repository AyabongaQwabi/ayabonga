The historical relationship between **Thembu chiefs** and the **San** (AbaThwa) at **Rhodana** (also spelled Rodana) represents a significant era of political integration and ritual partnership in the Cacadu (Glen Grey) basin starting in the early 1840s.

### **Establishment of Rhodana as a Strategic Sanctuary**
Rhodana was established in **1841–1842** when **Queen Nonesi** (the Great Wife of King Ngubengcuka) and her ward, the heir **Mtirara**, moved the Thembu "Great Place" to the foot of the mountains near the Cacadu River. 
*   **Geopolitical Purpose:** The move was a strategic relocation intended to place the Thembu royalty on the **frontline of defense** against British colonial expansion and pressures from neighboring Xhosa groups.
*   **San Presence:** The landscape was already inhabited by San communities who had occupied the rock shelters of the **Xonxa and Rhodana mountains** for millennia. Rather than displacing them, the Thembu engaged in a period of intensive **political integration** and **creolization**.

### **The Thembu-San Alliance: The Mqithi Clan**
The relationship between Queen Nonesi and the local San was anchored by the **Mqithi clan**, who were recognized as the "Royal San" of the Rhodana Great Place.
*   **High Status:** The San leader **Mqithi** was a central ally to the Queen Regent. His clan was granted land at **Qithi Village**, positioned directly adjacent to the Thembu royal residence—a marker of high rank comparable to a senior counselor or hereditary ritual specialist.
*   **Mutual Survival:** The San provided the Thembu with intelligence and **ritual power** (specifically rainmaking), while the Thembu provided the San with **protection and cattle**. 
*   **Linguistic Legacy:** The name "Mqithi" itself is considered a "linguistic fossil" of this merger, incorporating a standard Xhosa personal prefix with a traditional San palatal click.

### **Roles of the San in the Rhodana Polity**
The San were integrated into the Thembu social order through specialized roles that utilized their unique expertise:
*   **Rainmakers:** They provided spiritual services during droughts to ensure the survival of royal cattle, drawing on their perceived "ritual seniority" as the original inhabitants of the land.
*   **Frontier Scouts:** They used their superior knowledge of the rugged terrain to track colonial movements and enemy raiders.
*   **Elite Marksmen:** During the **War of Mlanjeni (1850–1853)**, San marksmen served as elite units alongside Thembu and Xhosa forces to defend the Xonxa and Rhodana mountains.
*   **Herbalists:** They provided medicinal knowledge derived from local flora.

### **The Defensive Ring of Villages**
Rhodana was surrounded by a defensive and agricultural ring of villages that integrated subject and allied clans:
*   **Rodana:** The seat of the Thembu Great Place under Nonesi's descendants.
*   **Qithi (Location 17/18):** The primary village for the integrated San followers.
*   **Mphothulo:** Associated with senior councilors and elite warriors.
*   **Ngqoko:** A village that later became a center for early Xhosa Christians.

### **Prominent Chiefs and Leaders at Rhodana**
*   **Queen Nonesi:** The "Great Wife" and diplomat who resisted colonial land-hunger for decades until her forced deportation in **1880**.
*   **King Mtirara:** The ward of Nonesi and heir to the Thembu throne who settled at Rhodana in 1841.
*   **Chief Mgudlwa (Mgudhluwa):** A central figure in the White Kei region whose territory was a primary contact zone; he integrated San marksmen into his regiments during the 1850s.
*   **Chief Jumba:** A senior Thembu chief whose household included San clients like **Silayi**, who provided crucial testimony regarding the integration of San bands into Xhosa society.
*   **Mqithi:** The San captain whose lineage remained custodians of the ritual landscape at Rhodana long after the initial settlement.
